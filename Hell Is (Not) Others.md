Vikas was mad. We don't mean just now, in this moment. With Vikas, being mad had in recent times become much like breathing. Like a crocodile, it quietly liked just beneath the calm surface he usually put on, until he and it would snap when one least expected it.

It would be unfair to lay it all on Vikas though. He had not been this way always. He was a friendly lad, a fun and outgoing young man, a gentle and kind lover and not so long ago a proud and doting dad to his baby daughter. But time changes all, and we all change with time. His recent unemployment planted a seed of bitterness, and it grew and rose alongside prices and inflation. 

His bubbling emotions needed venting and these days one could often find him holding placards and raising slogans, his voice thundering among the throngs. His voice drowned out all noise, including the distant calls of possible opportunities. Someone must surely be to blame.. and he had picked his them.

But one should not go to bed mad, it is often said. Perhaps it should also be said it is best not to get on a two wheeler mad. Had that been a popular saying, and had Vikas been cognisant of it, one could imagine he might have a avoided running his bike into the small pile of refuse discarded casually along the side for the sake of road improvements, and thereby not be flung off it.

If only the fates were always so kind, we might have less sorrow and lesser art. But that is not to be, and the doctors were already sorry the moment they saw him being wheeled in. And as his eyes closed for the last time, Vikas went from being blinded by anger to being blinded to it all.

Vikas startled awake. 'What in the ..!' he remembered speeding to his next protest, speeding around the street corner, and speeding towards the tarmac as he was launched from his seat. But he did not remember speeding into this large, burning, sweltering hall at any moment. 

And yet he was right in the middle of it: a large cavernous hall hewn much like a cave, from nature and fury, literally ablaze with flames licking its thick blood red columns. The welcome chamber of Naraka stood deserted but for him and the roaring flames. Vikas blinked in confusion, the Yama-doothas who were to welcome him to the rest of his existence were nowhere to be seen. The Bhoothas of those who blazed the trail he now trode, none were to be seen anywhere.

Disoriented, Vikas stumbled around looking for an exit. Pushing with all his might against the large stone doors, he stumbled awkwardly into the blazing foot trails of Hell. And.. there was not a soul (if you can pardon the pun) in sight. Where were his soon to be fellow denizens of hell? Vikas pondered.

A voice boomed in the distance, and was answered by the lowdest crowd Vikas had ever heard. Kindred spirits know each other no matter what they are, and the rumble of discontent and discord being all to familiar, Vikas was naturally drawn to the large square where the hubbub seemed to be centered.

A large Bootha was roaring into his megaphone: 'Who was it who built all there is ? whose blood and sweat gave form to these mighty vistas and wonders of Hell? Who worked tirelessly, eon after eon, time after endless time, to protect and nurture our home, our Hell?' he bellowed. 'It was Us! the Dead! We made it all possible. From the time we first came here till now several ages afterwards, it is we who have toiled and tolled for the prosperity and progress of Hell!'

'And yet, what of us today?' the Bootha continued with ferocity. 'We scrimp and scramble for scraps and pittances. They come in throngs my friends, yes they do, day after day after day, without abatement ! They come and they crowd  our fair home, reaping the benefits of that which we have sown! Yes my friends, it is the Living! the Living who recklessly die and deluge us, the Living who take what should have been ours!'

Vikas, who was just emerging from the corner onto the square ,froze. All of this.. sounded fairly familiar. One could even say the oration was a more forceful paraphrasing of his own slogans from not so long ago in the world of the Living.

'And what are we to do? What do we hear when we raise this rightful concern to the Almighty devas? We are told such is the way of nature! that it is Divine will that we shall continue to welcome these undeserving , unkempt, unrepentant, iniquitous Living whenever they die! Well I say my friends: No Longer! No longer shall we welcome the Living along us whenever they decese! Join me and lend me your arms.. to chase out them from amongst our midst.. starting with him!' the Bhootha pointed in Vikas's direction.

And as the crowed swiftly turned toward, and on, him Vikas turned as well. Off he took, his feet pounding the pavement stones as if they were on fire, which was literally true for some of them. The crowd thundered right on his heels. 'Hell is for the Dead!' 'The Living should get the Hell out!' slogans boomeranged at him faster than the crowd.

Vikas sprinted, barely catching his breath, back towards the large gates of hell. And even as the crowd closed in, as he sprang through the gates, there lingered a faint thought at the back of his mind: 'wait.. am I also not Dead too? what am I running for!'

Vikas jerking awake and sitting upright nearly proved the end of the doctor and nurse who had just attended him. 'Wait, how are you sitting up? I swear I just thought you were dead !' exclaimed she. 'Well doctor..' Vikas replied, awoken to new life. 'Hell was full of people who did not want to share!'
