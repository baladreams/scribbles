Vikash was mad. We don't meant just now, in this moment. With Vikash, being mad had in recent times become much like breathing. Like a crocodile, it quietly liked just beneath the calm surface he usually put on, until he and it would snap when one least expected it.

It would be unfair to lay it all on Vikash though. He had not been this way always. He was a friendly lad, a fun and outgoing young man, a gently and kind lover and not so long ago a proud and doting dad to his baby daughter. But time changes all, and we change it time. His recent joblessness had planted a seed of bitterness, and it grew and rose alongside prices and inflation. 

His bubbling emotions needed venting and these days one could often find him holding placards and raising slogans, his voice thundering even among the throngs. And his own voice drowned out all noise, including the distant calls of possible opportunities. Someone must surely be to blame.. and he had picked his cause.

But one should not go to bed mad, it is often said. Perhaps it should also be said it is best not to be on a two wheeler mad. Had that been a popular saying, and had Vikas been cognisant of it, one could imagine he might have a avoided running his bike into the small pile of refuse discarded casually along the side for the sake of improving the roads, thereby not being flung off it.

If only the fates were always kind, we might have less sorrow and lesser art. But that is not to be, and the doctors were already sorry the moment they saw him being wheeled in. And even as his eyes closed for the last time, Vikas went from being blinded by anger to be blinded to it all.

Vikas jerked awake. 'What in the ..!' he remembered speeding to his next protest, speeding around the street corner , and speeding towards the tarmac as he was launched from his seat. But he did not remember speeding into this large , burning sweltering hall at any moment. 

And yet he was right in the middle of it:a large cavernous hall hewn much like a cave of nature and fury, literally ablaze with flames licking its think blood red columns. The welcome chamber of Yamaloga stood deserted but for him and the roaring flames. Vikas blinked in confusion, the Yama-doothas who were to welcome him to the rest of his existence were nowhere to be seen. The Yakshas, the Rakshasas, the Boothas of those who blazed the trail he now trode , none were to be seen anywhere.

Disoriented, Vikas stumbled around looking for an exit. Pushing with all his might against the large stone doors, he stumbled awkwardly into the blazing foot trails of Hell. And.. there was not a soul (if you can pardon the pun) in sight. Where we his soon to be fellow denizens of hell? Vikas pondered.

A voice boomed in the distance, and was andewer by the lowest crowd Vikas had ever heard. Kindred spirits know each other no matter what they are , and the rumble if discontent and discord being all to familiar to Vikas, be was naturally drawn to the large square in the center where the hubbub seemed to be centered.

A large Devil was roaring into his megaphone: 'Who was it who built all there is to see here ? whose blood and sweat gave form to these mighty palaces and cities if Hell? Who had worked tirelessly, eon after eon, time after endless time, to protect and nurture our home, our Hell?' he bellowed. 'It was Us! the Dead and the Devils! We made it all possible. From the time we first came here till now several ages afterwards, it is we who have toiled and tolled for the prosperity and progress of Hell!'

One could hardly reason against this, one can hardly reason against any truth.

'And yet, what of us today ?' he continued with ferocity. 'We fight and scramble for scraps and pittances. They come in throngs my friends, yes they do, every day and every day after that without abatement ! They come and they crowd  our fair home, reaping the benefits of that which we have sown! Yes my friends, it is the Living! the Living who recklessly for every day and come here and stay endlessly, taking what should have been ours!'

Vikas stood frozen to his spot just as he was emerging into the crowd. All of this.. sounded fairly familiar to him. After all, one could even say the oration was a more forceful paraphrasing of his own slogans he had been chanting now so long ago in the world of the Living.

'And what are we to do? What do we hear when we raise this rightful concern to the Almighty devas? We are told such is the way of nature! that it is Divine will that we shall continue to welcome these undeserving , unkempt, unrepentant, iniquitous creatures whenever they die! The Dead here have been with us for centuries, they have earned the right which these recently living have not ! Well I say my friends: No Longer ! No longer shall we welcome the Living along us whenever they decese! Join me and let me your arms.. to chase out these underserving amongst our midst.. starting with him!' the devil pointed one large , well nailed finger in his direction.

And as the crowed quickly turned toward, and on, him Vikas too swiftly turned. And in moments, off he took, his feet pounding the pavement stones as if they were in fire, which was literally true for some of them. The crowd thundered right on his heels: 'Hell is for the Dead!' 'The Living should get the Hell out!' slogans chased him down faster than the crowd.

Vikas sprinted , not even bothering to catch his breath anymore , back towards the large gates if hell. And even as the crowd neared, and he sprang through the gates there lingered a faint thought at the back of his mind: 'wait.. am I also not dead ? what am I running for !'
